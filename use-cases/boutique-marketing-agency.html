Boutique Marketing Agency

From market signals to coordinated campaigns

A 12-person agency needed to punch above its weight against larger competitors. Nora captures how their senior strategists connect market intelligence to campaign decisions, then coordinates research, creative briefs, client approvals, and performance tracking in one governed workflow.

The challenge

The agency's competitive advantage lived in their senior strategists' heads — pattern recognition built over years of campaign work. When a strategist left, that intelligence left with them. Junior team members couldn't access the decision frameworks that made campaigns successful.

Specific pain points

  • Fragmented context: Market research in one tool, client history in email, campaign performance in spreadsheets, creative briefs in Google Docs.
  • Inconsistent quality: Campaigns led by seniors outperformed junior-led work by 3–5x on key metrics, but seniors were bottlenecked across 8–10 concurrent clients.
  • Lost learning: Post-campaign retros happened sporadically and weren't connected to future planning. The same mistakes repeated across different account teams.
  • Client visibility gaps: Clients couldn't see the strategic reasoning behind campaign decisions, only the outputs. This led to revision cycles and scope creep.

How Nora works here

1

Capture expert decisions

Senior strategist reviews market research and client context. Nora prompts for decision reasoning: "Why this audience segment?" "What creative direction and why?" "What channels and budget split?" Each answer is tagged with evidence links and confidence levels.

2

Structure into patterns

Nora extracts recurring decision patterns: "B2B SaaS launches → LinkedIn + content marketing, 60/40 split, 90-day runway." "DTC beauty → influencer seeding first, paid social second, 45-day test window." Patterns become reusable templates with guardrails.

3

Coordinate AI agents

Once the strategist approves the direction, Nora coordinates bounded agents: research agent gathers competitive intel, brief agent drafts the creative brief with embedded context, media agent builds the channel plan with budget allocations. Each agent works within defined boundaries.

4

Human approval gates

Critical decisions pause for human review: creative brief before client presentation, budget allocation before media buy, channel mix before launch. The strategist sees the full evidence trail — sources, reasoning, agent outputs — in one view.

5

Execute and track

Approved workflows connect to business tools: Google Docs for creative briefs, Google Sheets for media plans, Slack for team notifications, email for client updates. Every action is logged with who approved what and when.

6

Capture outcomes and learn

Campaign performance data flows back: CTR, conversion rate, CPA, ROAS. Nora links outcomes to the original decisions and patterns. The next campaign starts with: "Similar client in Q2 achieved 2.3% CTR with this approach — here's what we'd adjust."

Concrete outputs

Every campaign workflow produces auditable artifacts:

  • Strategic Brief Market context, audience definition, creative direction, channel strategy, budget allocation, success metrics — with reasoning and evidence linked throughout.
  • Creative Brief Auto-generated from strategic decisions, includes campaign goals, target audience, key messages, tone, deliverables, timeline.
  • Media Plan Channel-by-channel budget split, flight dates, targeting parameters, creative specs, performance benchmarks.
  • Client Presentation Slide deck with strategic rationale, competitive context, proposed approach, expected outcomes, investment breakdown.
  • Performance Report Actuals vs. benchmarks, learnings, recommended adjustments for next cycle.
  • Decision Journal Append-only log of all decisions, approvers, timestamps, and evidence — searchable for future retros or client questions.

Workflow profile

  • Team size: 12 people (3 seniors, 5 juniors, 4 operations)
  • Clients: 8–10 concurrent B2B/DTC engagements
  • Cycle time: 2.5 weeks brief-to-launch (was 4 weeks)
  • Decision gates: 4 per campaign (strategy, creative, media, launch)
  • Artifacts per campaign: 6+ auditable outputs

What Nora coordinates

  • Market research aggregation
  • Competitive intelligence
  • Strategic decision capture
  • Creative brief generation
  • Media plan assembly
  • Client presentation drafting
  • Performance tracking
  • Learning loop updates

Human authority

Nora does not make strategic decisions. It captures them, structures them, and ensures they execute consistently. The senior strategist owns:

  • Audience definition
  • Creative direction
  • Channel selection
  • Budget allocation
  • Client communication

Learning loop

Every campaign outcome feeds back:

  • Performance vs. benchmark
  • What worked / what didn't
  • Pattern adjustments
  • Template refinements
  • Guardrail updates

The next campaign starts smarter than the last one ended.
